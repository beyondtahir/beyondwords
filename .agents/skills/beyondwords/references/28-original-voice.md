# Original voice that survives a whole book

Apply with every supported host model. These are portable instructions, not a claim that all hosts have been tested. Use the selected guide persona/sample and existing versioned fiction/nonfiction/visual bible. Do not invent a second intake or a famous-author impersonation.

## Build the voice from the actual story

Read the accepted reader promise, researched genre expectations, concept and outline. The title alone cannot determine a voice. Offer two or three concise original directions grounded in this story's emotional stakes, setting and intended reader. Ask about missing preferences using short sample choices; users do not need technical vocabulary.

Define reader, promise, viewpoint, tense, narrative distance, sentence rhythm, diction, dialogue, imagery, humor, emotional register and what to avoid. Make these concrete. For example: “close third-person, past tense; a suspicious but observant protagonist; concrete details about a failing seaside workshop; dry humor under pressure; shorter clauses during danger; no narrator explaining every emotion.” Treat this as an invented style proposal until the author accepts the sample, not market evidence.

Give major characters different wants, blind spots, vocabulary and ways of evading questions. Track how their choices change relationships. Fantasy needs coherent constraints and costs; mystery needs fair clues and causal solutions; romance needs earned emotional turns and consent appropriate to the story. A voice card cannot compensate for an implausible plot. Visual/activity books need a consistent art direction and useful instructions instead of novelistic filler.

Draft a short original sample, usually 300–600 words or the equivalent spread/scene. Let the author choose/revise the direction, save its exact accepted hash through the guide, then carry the voice into the book plan/bible. Broad craft traits are useful inspiration; do not copy distinctive passages, fabricate experiences or promise an indistinguishable living-author imitation. Human-sounding writing does not change provenance or remove required AI disclosure.

## Draft and edit with intent

Before each chapter, retrieve `writing-context`, current bible, relevant prior text and open story threads. Write against that chapter's purpose and the accepted sample. Prefer concrete actions, specific sensory details, subtext and earned transitions. Vary rhythm with the scene; omit generic motivational endings, repeated summaries, overexplained feelings and decorative metaphors that do not belong to the viewpoint.

Default to restrained em/en dashes in prose. Prefer a period, comma, colon or a rewritten sentence when clearer. Preserve legitimate hyphenated words, ranges and purposeful interrupted dialogue. Do not mechanically replace punctuation or flatten every character into the same clipped style. Respect an author's explicit punctuation preference. No punctuation count proves AI authorship.

Run `publishing-check` task `voice` with `text`, explicit `synthetic` and the structured `persona` above plus `sample_sha256`. It reports missing persona fields, em/en/spaced-hyphen counts, exact repeated paragraphs and three-word paragraph openings repeated at least three times. Counts are editorial prompts, not automatic defects. It neither rewrites nor awards a “human” score. Its sample hash does not itself establish acceptance; use the existing guide record.

Read the flagged sentences in context, revise only what improves meaning, read dialogue aloud and compare a new excerpt with the accepted sample. Use the actual full-text `editorial` packet/check/rewrite/recheck loop, continuity ledger and source checks. Record why purposeful repetition was retained. Do not claim a model critique is an independent editor or reader. Whole-book editorial and real-reader evaluations remain release gates.
