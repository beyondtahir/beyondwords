# Niche research and BSR

Default to five source-linked choices in conversation; keep detailed research records internally. Write an extended Opportunity Brief only when requested. Match opportunities to the user's knowledge, reachable audience, budget and production capacity.

Research legitimate sources for observable demand, comparable books, reader needs, competition and practical differentiation. Record sampling method, market, dates, sponsored/organic positions where visible, missing fields and selection limitations. Do not assume search result counts measure all competition. Weak competition can mean weak demand; existing competition alone does not prove profitable entry.

Data access is a release dependency. Use approved/licensed sources, appropriately permitted host browsing, authorized files and user-provided records. A browser or an affiliate API key does not grant unrestricted commercial collection rights. Without suitable access, state the limitation and accept an authorized observation/import. Do not fabricate a live niche database or historic charts.

For live website research, default to the normal interactive browser actually available in the current host. Navigate observed links and verify the rendered page, selected edition and marketplace. Use the web reader as a backup when the browser is unavailable or has a technical failure; search can discover URLs but old search snippets cannot replace current page observations. For a reader timeout or HTTP 503, check the normal browser once if it has not already been tried before reporting that live access is unavailable. Record each route's actual result; do not invent the cause of a failure.

Stop on an explicit access denial, login-only restriction or CAPTCHA; switching tools must not bypass it. If supported routes cannot supply the evidence, retain UNKNOWN and use a permitted alternate source or authorized import. Browser access verifies only this source and session, not every host or future availability. Record displayed currency and delivery region separately from marketplace; local-currency or international-delivery prices are not automatically domestic list-price or royalty inputs.

Each rank observation needs: product identifier; marketplace; store; format; edition verified; store/category scope; category when applicable; paid/free list; rank or missing reason; observation time; evidence reference; collection/access basis. Preserve source lineage. Group comparable observations before interpreting them. Book category ranks can aggregate formats: do not mistake category ranks for format-specific overall rank.

Run the bundled BSR validator and comparability helper for supplied records. They validate structure/context, not authenticity. Do not convert a rank into exact units or earnings. Historical analysis requires real history. Any later sales estimator must disclose the marketplace/format model, calibration data, evaluation and uncertainty; otherwise disable it.

Review themes require a permitted source and sampling limits. Avoid republishing lengthy reviews or competitors' text; extract ideas and needs, not a copied outline. Low reviews plus a good rank is an observation, not proof of an easy niche.

## Inspect samples before recommending

For the books supporting a niche or gap recommendation, open the permitted retailer reading sample, publisher preview or authorized supplied excerpt. Verify its title, edition/format and source. Visually inspect actual pages for illustrated, coloring and activity books; image alt text and promotional descriptions alone do not establish interior quality. Record the inspection date, source URL, access basis, preview type, exactly which pages/sections were viewed, and any unreadable or omitted pages. A back-cover collage or publisher image is promotional coverage, not a complete interior review. If a sample is unavailable, keep the candidate provisional and explain the remaining evidence need; do not imply the book was read.

Compare reader promise and delivered content. For fiction inspect the available opening, voice, pacing and character setup; for nonfiction inspect contents, explanations, examples and progression; for visual/activity books inspect line clarity, fillable areas, complexity, composition variety, instructions, margins and sample-page consistency. Physical paper performance and whole-book repetition require appropriate proofs or broader access; a digital preview cannot establish them. Compare relevant established and less-established competitors where available, and record selection bias.

Connect each proposed gap to a specific sample observation and any independently read review or real-reader evidence. Keep observations, creative interpretations and tests separate. Check whether competitors already provide the feature; counterevidence must revise or remove the gap. Absence from a limited preview is not absence from the complete book. Turn surviving hypotheses into an original short prototype brief with reader, improvement, production constraints and an observable reader/quality test before extensive creation. Preserve concise original notes and permitted evidence, not copied competitor art, prose or distinctive layouts. A proposed improvement still requires author choice and real-reader validation; neither sample access nor a rank establishes demand for it.

## Five researched choices, then the author's selection

With no chosen niche, investigate five distinct niches and give one concrete book idea in each: five niches and five ideas in total. A niche identifies a reader/buyer and a particular need or reading experience; five cosmetic themes for essentially the same product are not five different niches. Compare feasible fiction, nonfiction and visual routes where relevant to intake, without forcing irrelevant types into the shortlist.

With a chosen niche, investigate five meaningfully different ideas inside it. Distinguish the reader problem, promise, story experience or activity design, not just the title. A broad label such as fantasy still needs specific reader-facing directions. Do not reopen unrelated niches or turn an expressed interest into a selected niche. When the author selects a niche from the initial five, deepen its research into five ideas, retaining the initial concept if the evidence still supports it. Reuse current evidence rather than repeating collection solely to fill a stage.

Research every candidate before describing it as supported. Use this sequence to move from a topic to a defensible original idea:

1. **Narrow the reader and search.** Turn broad topics into reader-specific search phrases. Generated phrases are starting hypotheses; search current permitted marketplace results for actual comparable books, not just a bestseller list. Record organic/sponsored placement where visible and include established and less-established titles where available.
2. **Check the pattern across books.** Record multiple independent works, selected editions/formats, current ranks, prices and review counts. Apply the three-works/below-80,000 comparable-rank screen below when its inputs are available; preserve pass/fail/unknown. Low review counts or poor ratings can identify books to investigate, but do not establish easy entry, low competition or the cause of sales performance.
3. **Read the reader evidence.** Inspect accessible review text, including positive, mixed and critical feedback. Separate what buyers value from what frustrates them. Record recurring themes with the actual number of reviewed comments, dates and selection limitations; one complaint is an individual observation, not a repeated pattern. Do not invent a theme from the average star rating.
4. **Inspect delivery in the samples.** Check permitted previews/contents against those themes: useful coverage, missing explanations, filler, progression, voice/pacing or art/activity design as relevant. Preserve successful reader expectations as well as weaknesses. Follow the coverage rules above; a preview omission is not proof of a whole-book omission. Search for competing books that already address the proposed improvement.
5. **Turn the surviving need into an original book.** Name the specific reader, desired outcome/experience, observed limitation and proposed improvement. For instructional nonfiction, connect the reader's starting problem to a practical end result; chapters later take responsibility for the necessary steps. For fiction and visual books, specify the desired story or activity experience instead of forcing a how-to structure. Create an original approach; do not copy competitor prose, artwork or distinctive organization. Reject, narrow or revise a gap when contrary evidence undermines it.
6. **Check whether we can deliver it.** Compare production skill, reader testing, format eligibility, actual pricing/cost inputs and the author's constraints. Define a small prototype and observable test before commissioning the full book. A higher price needs a defensible reader benefit and cost basis; it is not automatically a higher profit. Keep absent evidence explicit and explain why this candidate is worth testing relative to the alternatives.

Keep an internal trace for every proposed gap: reader need → source observation → sample/review coverage → contrary evidence → original design response → next test. This is a research note, not a new tool schema. A missing preview or relevant review makes that part of the case incomplete, not negative evidence about the book. These steps support a research judgment; only actual reader/purchase evidence can test acceptance, and none guarantees future sales.

The first five-option comparison should normally fit in roughly 350–450 words total. Use five short numbered choices and one brief recommendation/decision question. Put shared limitations and unknown cost inputs once, not under every choice. Retain a relevant source link, the gap/design connection, the material counterevidence and the first test in each choice; expand details for the selected idea or on request. Do not hide a disqualifying limitation to meet a word target. Full source counts, inspected passages, alternative explanations and prototype procedures belong in the saved research notes. The following are the information to cover, not five subheadings to repeat inside every option:

- **Who and what:** niche, specific reader/buyer, concrete original book concept, reader promise and suitable format. For a chosen niche, name the distinct idea instead.
- **Why investigate it:** dated, linked marketplace observations and specific sample/review findings. Describe what was actually inspected, not a whole-book assessment from a few pages.
- **Gap → design:** the unmet need or underserved experience suggested by those observations, then what the proposed book would do differently. Separate observed facts from interpretation. For fiction this can be a desired reading experience, not a claim that a popular story is defective.
- **Fit and tradeoffs:** author input/expertise, feasible production route, effort and cost basis, contrary evidence, competition and remaining unknowns. Estimate effort from the planned prototype where possible; mark unknown amounts rather than assuming them. Do not force an unsuitable Kindle edition merely because both formats were requested.
- **Next test:** the smallest useful original sample and an observable reader or quality test, with the result that would support revision, proceeding or stopping. Describe the idea as worth testing when evidence supports that, not commercially proven.

After the five options, identify the one to test first and explain the tradeoff against the others using the author's constraints. Ask one decision: choose an option or ask to refine the comparison. Record only the author's actual selection or a choice within explicit delegated scope. Selection permits the next development step; it does not establish reader validation, editorial approval or readiness to publish.

Five is the default research target, not permission to manufacture five winners. If only three cases have support, state that two remain unfinished and continue permitted research. If access, evidence or author constraints genuinely prevent completion, report the exact limitation and the next evidence route; keep unsupported candidates labelled provisional. Do not silently present a shorter list as a finished five-option comparison. Follow an explicit author request to narrow the scope, without disguising that as complete research of five options. More than five may be compared when requested; the guide stores at most five per choice set, so agree the current set without silently replacing saved options or decisions.

Opportunity Brief sections, when requested: proposed buyer/reader; evidence; alternatives; competitive gap; original angle; author fit; expected production effort; economics assumptions; risks; missing evidence; recommendation (PROCEED, TEST_FIRST, REWORK or DO_NOT_PUBLISH_CURRENT_VERSION).

AI-generated reader personas are critique aids, not market research participants. Only real, consented feedback is customer validation. Keep rights/policy blockers separate from commercial judgments.

Use research-desk and market-screen from 19-specialist-tools.md. A configurable hypothesis asks whether at least three distinct same-niche works have comparable paid overall bookstore ranks below 80,000. Verify exact editions, dates and context; deduplicate formats. Passing only supports further investigation. It never supplies sales, royalties, profitability or a probability of success.
