# Launch, sales and cost discipline

Prepare truthful descriptions, samples, author information, social assets, reader feedback plans and budget-limited experiments. Check that the manuscript delivers each promise in the listing. Do not generate fake testimonials or unearned badges.

Early-reader copies must not require favorable reviews or involve reciprocal/rewarded manipulation. Recheck current review rules. Customer contact and marketing consent are separate from a purchase. External messages or campaign launches need explicit authorization.

Before paid generation or advertising, show estimated cost and reserve an agreed budget. Use cached authorized research, approved assets and targeted revisions. Do not repeatedly regenerate a whole book to fix one section. A host subscription may not include separate model, image, search or data-provider charges.

Use actual author receipts/royalties rather than retail gross price for profitability. Separate observed receipts, pending estimates, ad spend, printing, platform fees, refunds, tax assumptions and production costs. Avoid double-counting costs already deducted from receipts. Do not combine different currencies without an explicit dated FX source.

The bundled economics helper calculates contribution and break-even under supplied assumptions. It does not know current royalties, payment eligibility or future demand. Negative/zero contribution has no finite break-even output. A breakeven quantity is not a sales prediction.

Post-launch analysis: use authorized publisher/ad reports; preserve reporting dates, currency, return delays and attribution limitations. Discuss causal uncertainty. Optimize from real results, not simulated persona approval. The bundled Sponsored Products connector supports documented scoped actions with actual account onboarding and acceptance still required. Other integrations depend on available authorized tools. Follow [the post-publication ads manager](29-amazon-ads-manager.md) for access, budgets, launch, analysis and verified monitoring.
