# Style Profiles — Persona/Voice System per Book Type

Pick one profile at book-spec time and hold it for the whole manuscript. Each profile lists structure, tone, pacing, and POV/tense norms, plus generic structural patterns common to famous books in the category (described, not copied).

## Contents
1. How-to non-fiction
2. Kids' picture book
3. Middle-grade novel
4. Adult novel
5. Cookbook
6. Workbook / journal

## 1. How-to non-fiction

- **Structure:** Point A → Point B; 8–12 chapters × ~3 subsections; each chapter ends in a key-takeaway box. Intro promises the transformation; conclusion recaps and points forward.
- **Tone:** "teaching a friend" — direct, warm, specific. Second person ("you"). No lecturing.
- **Pacing:** 1,000–2,000 words per section; one actionable step per section; a story or concrete example per chapter.
- **POV/tense:** second person, present tense for instructions; past tense for stories.
- **Classic pattern:** problem → false solutions → the method in numbered steps → obstacle handling → maintenance plan.

## 2. Kids' picture book

- **Structure:** 24–32 pages, one scene per page or spread; setup → problem → three attempts (rule of three) → resolution → quiet ending.
- **Tone:** warm, rhythmic, read-aloud cadence; repetition with variation.
- **Pacing:** 30–80 words per page; total 400–800 words.
- **POV/tense:** third person, past tense (or present for concept books).
- **Craft notes:** character consistency matters more than prose style; illustration notes per page; the pictures carry half the story — write what the art cannot show.

## 3. Middle-grade novel

- **Structure:** 12–20 chapters, 20,000–40,000 words; clear quest/goal; chapters end on small hooks.
- **Tone:** honest, funny, never talks down; big feelings handled plainly.
- **Pacing:** fast scenes, short chapters, cliffhanger chapter endings.
- **POV/tense:** first person past or close third past.
- **Classic pattern:** ordinary world → inciting problem → escalating attempts → low point → self-driven win (the kid solves it, not the adults).

## 4. Adult novel

- **Structure:** 25–60 chapters, 70,000–100,000 words; act structure or hero's-journey skeleton; scene/sequel rhythm.
- **Tone:** genre-dependent; voice is the product — lock a sample page early and imitate it.
- **Pacing:** alternate tension and release; every scene turns (goal → conflict → change).
- **POV/tense:** genre norms (thriller: third past or first present; romance: dual first past; fantasy: third past).
- **Continuity:** use the story bible (`story-bible.md`) — immutable facts, accept-before-canon drafting.

## 5. Cookbook

- **Structure:** themed chapters (breakfasts, weeknight, celebrations); intro per chapter; recipes in strict template (yield, time, ingredients in order of use, numbered steps, variation box).
- **Tone:** encouraging, sensory, practical.
- **Pacing:** 100–300 words of headnote per recipe.
- **POV/tense:** imperative steps ("Whisk…"), present-tense headnotes.
- **Craft notes:** consistency of measurement units; substitutions and dietary flags per recipe.

## 6. Workbook / journal

- **Structure:** short teaching section → prompt pages → space to write; weekly or themed units.
- **Tone:** gentle coach; questions, not lectures.
- **Pacing:** 200–500 words of teaching per unit; the reader does the rest.
- **POV/tense:** second person, present.
- **Craft notes:** page-level layout is the product; prompts must be specific ("List the three bills that arrive before the 15th"), not vague ("Reflect on money").
