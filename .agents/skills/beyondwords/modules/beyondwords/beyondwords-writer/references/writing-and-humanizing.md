# Writing and editing for real readers

Start with the reader's problem or desired experience, the buyer's expectations and the author's genuine contribution. Use an approved outline and a limited sample to test the promise before expanding a book. Chapter count, section length and ending structure depend on genre and purpose; no universal template establishes quality.

Use only authorized competitor samples to understand conventions and identify gaps. Develop original examples, structure, language and art. Never invent personal experiences or qualifications. Label hypothetical examples and distinguish sourced claims from creative invention.

Edit for specific detail, clear reasoning, practical usefulness where appropriate, continuity, variation and absence of repeated filler. Keep a claim/source ledger, check contradictions and remove unsupported claims. A lint score is a heuristic diagnostic, not proof of human authorship or good writing. No detector-evasion or retailer-detection claim is supported.

Model critique can assist revisions but does not replace independent editorial and actual-reader evaluations. Preserve approved versions and record exact artifacts reviewed. Use the current writing-context, versioned chapter and editorial tools; independent reader evaluation remains separate.
